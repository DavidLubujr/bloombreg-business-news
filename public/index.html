No credentials are collected, no downloads are triggered, and this exercise does not compromise your device.

A convincing headline made this link tempting. Attackers use familiar brands, exciting news and curiosity to encourage clicks before you check the destination.

Pause. Inspect. Verify. Then click.

01 / PAUSE

Question the hook.

Unexpected milestones, prizes and urgent requests can be bait. A familiar name does not prove a message is genuine.

02 / INSPECT

Check the destination.

Hover on desktop or press and hold on mobile to preview the URL. Read the actual domain carefully. A padlock alone does not prove a site is trustworthy.

03 / VERIFY

Use a trusted route.

Open your usual company portal or a saved bookmark to verify the news. Report suspicious messages through your organisation’s established security channel.

One quick check

An exciting company headline arrives with an unfamiliar link. What should you do?

If you clicked a suspicious link in real life: close the page and report it promptly. If you entered a password, change it through the official service and tell IT/security. If anything downloaded, avoid opening it and seek IT guidance.
